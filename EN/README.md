# AI Landmark Papers: From Foundations to the Frontier — Contents

> A long-term collection project commissioned by Ezra on 2026-10-19: the landmark papers of AI history, organized by era into study materials. Each entry gives the original title, authors and year, a one-line statement of the paper's significance, the abstract, a guide to the key ideas, the paper's relation to the work before and after it, and a link to the original. Papers marked ★ are slated for full-text translation (in the Chinese edition); the rest appear as translated reading guides. This English edition mirrors the Chinese edition volume for volume; abstracts are quoted verbatim from the original papers where available, each with its source noted.

## Editorial conventions

- Abstracts are quoted verbatim from the original papers (arXiv abstract pages, publisher pages, PubMed), with the source and version noted in each entry; they are never back-translations of the Chinese.
- Early papers that have no abstract in the modern sense (1943/1950/1958) say so plainly in their entries; a reading guide takes the abstract's place and is never passed off as one.
- Where the arXiv preprint and the published version differ (BatchNorm, Latent Diffusion, DeepSeek-R1, etc.), the version used is the one stated in the entry, and the difference is noted.
- ★ marks the first-tier papers slated for full-text translation: backpropagation, AlexNet, Bahdanau attention, ResNet, Attention Is All You Need, BERT, GPT-3, InstructGPT, and Attention Residuals — nine in the guides; on 2026-10-20 Ezra added AlphaGo, DeepSeek-R1, and Turing 1950 to the full-translation queue, expanding it to twelve.
